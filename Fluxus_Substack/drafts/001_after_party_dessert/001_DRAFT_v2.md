## 🔓 墙外（免费预览）

### The leaders are having dessert

Software closed last week up **6.88%**. The Russell 2000 closed **down 0.75%**. Those are the same five sessions.

The index looked fine because fewer and fewer names were carrying it — the equal-weight S&P **fell 0.56%** in a week the cap-weighted S&P **rose 0.77%**. When those two disagree by that much, the average stock is not having the week the headline says it is.

That is what a party looks like from the inside once it starts to end. The room is emptying. The people still at the table are on dessert.

I spent the week buying dessert — five new positions, four of them opened on the same day. Below is the number that made me do it, what it cost, and the three things I need to see before I do anything else.

## What changed

**Net advances went from 1,431 to −1,408.**

One reading is an anecdote, so here is the whole ladder for the same five sessions — top to bottom, largest to smallest:

| | last week |
|---|---:|
| **IGV** (software) | **+6.88%** |
| XLK (tech) | +3.13% |
| QQQ | +1.43% |
| SPY | +0.77% |
| DIA | +0.26% |
| **RSP** (equal-weight S&P) | **−0.56%** |
| MDY (mid caps) | −0.56% |
| **IWM** (small caps) | **−0.75%** |

**Read it from both ends.** The top is one narrow slice of software having an excellent week. The bottom is most of the market going quietly backwards. The gap between SPY and its own equal-weight twin — **1.33 points in five sessions** — is the whole story compressed into two numbers.

**I have four breadth readings and I check all four, including the ones that disagree with me.** This week they do not disagree:

| reading | direction |
|---|---|
| Share above the 50-day (46.6, 3rd percentile of 90 days) | deteriorating |
| Net advances, 10-day (−452.5, 0th percentile) | deteriorating |
| New lows vs new highs (15 lows vs 8 highs) | deteriorating |
| McClellan oscillator (−23.1) | deteriorating |

⚠️ **The fourth one only agrees because we found a bug in it this weekend.** Our high/low reading had been counting SPACs — 58 of the 66 names it called 52-week highs were shell companies. On the standard exchange definition the count is 8 highs against 15 lows. **I had this reading backwards twice before I had it right,** and I would rather tell you that than quietly publish the version that agrees with me.

**Now the part that names the week.** Split the same theme data by lookback:

| window | share of themes lagging |
|---|---:|
| **2 weeks** | **57.1%** (+21.4pp in five sessions) |
| 4–10 weeks | 32–36% |
| 3 months | 20.0%, and falling |

**The short window is wrecked. The long window has not moved.** That is not a contradiction — it is a sequence. The damage starts at the front, and the things that have been leading for months are still leading, for now. Dessert.

**So here is what I actually did.** I bought the part that is still leading, and I did it knowing the tape underneath it is getting worse:

| | opened | ticket | status |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **TEM** | Aug 24 · 67.25, stop 65.20 | 0.26% | open |
| **NOW** | Aug 26 · 132.89, stop 130.00 | 0.21% | trimmed Aug 27 at 137.50 · **+0.80R** banked |
| **SAIL** | Aug 26 · 20.11, stop 19.40 | 0.22% | open |
| **FIG** | Aug 26 · 28.64, stop 27.70 | 0.24% | open |
| **CRCL** | Aug 26 · 94.47, stop 91.40 | 0.38% | open |

**What it cost.** **1.31% of the account, added in three days** — and four of those five tickets were bought on a single day. If I am wrong about which end of that ladder matters, they are not five independent bets. They are one bet with five tickers on it, and it will fail as one.

## 🔓 墙外（免费预览）

### The leaders are having dessert

Software closed last week up **6.88%**. The Russell 2000 closed **down 0.75%**. Those are the same five sessions.

Then Friday happened. The Fed chair came out of Jackson Hole hawkish, the dollar and yields ripped, and the indexes faded a morning rally to close near the lows. The S&P gave up 0.25%. The Russell gave up **1.39%** — and lost its 8-, 21- and 50-day averages in a single session.

Software was the only thing still working.

That is what a party looks like once it has started to end. The room empties, and the people still at the table are on dessert. I spent the first half of the week buying dessert and the second half asking for the check. **I finished Friday with 48% of the book in cash.**

Below is what that cost.

## 🔒 墙内

## What changed

**Net advances went from 1,431 to −1,408.**

One reading is an anecdote, so here is the whole ladder:

| | last week |
|---|---:|
| **IGV** (software) | **+6.88%** |
| XLK (tech) | +3.13% |
| QQQ | +1.43% |
| SPY | +0.77% |
| DIA | +0.26% |
| **RSP** (equal-weight S&P) | **−0.56%** |
| MDY (mid caps) | −0.56% |
| **IWM** (small caps) | **−0.75%** |

**Read it from both ends.** The top is a narrow slice of software having an excellent week. The bottom is most of the market going quietly backwards. The gap between the S&P and its own equal-weight twin — **1.33 points in five sessions** — is the whole story in two numbers.

**I keep four breadth readings and I check all four, including the ones that disagree with me.** This week none of them disagreed: share of names above the 50-day sat in the 3rd percentile of 90 days, 10-day net advances in the 0th, the McClellan oscillator at −23, and new lows outnumbering new highs.

⚠️ **That fourth one only agrees because we found a bug in it this weekend.** Our high/low count had been including SPACs — **58 of the 66 names it called 52-week highs were shell companies.** On the standard exchange definition it is 8 highs against 15 lows. I had that reading backwards twice before I had it right, and I would rather tell you that than quietly publish the version that agrees with me.

**But the tell of the week was not the index. It was NVDA.** The company delivered a genuinely strong report, gapped up on heavy volume, filled the gap, and closed lower. Marvell beat *and* raised and finished −10.3%. The cybersecurity names gapped up on good numbers and reversed into overhead supply. **When a tape stops paying for good news, the character has changed** — that is worth more than any breadth number I just showed you.

**So here is what I actually did, in two halves.**

**First half: I bought the leaders of the leading themes.** Six tickets, all in software and enterprise. I gave each of them one to three days to tell me something.

**Only two answered.**

| opened | | held | result |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Aug 24 | **TEM** — 67.25, stop 65.20 | 3 days | **−0.0R** |
| Aug 24 | **MRNA** — 151.70, stop 140.00 | 1 day | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **NOW** (add) — 132.89, stop 130.00 | 1 day | **+2.7R** |
| Aug 26 | **FIG** — 28.64, stop 27.70 | still on | +0.2R |
| Aug 26 | **SAIL** — 20.11, stop raised to 21.11 | 3 days | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **CRCL** — 94.47, stop 91.40 | 1 day | **−0.6R** |

**Second half: I took the check.** Four of the six came back off the table within three days, and I raised cash from roughly 42% to **48.15%**.

**The one that worked is the one I want to explain, because it is the whole point of this letter.** NOW was my best answer of the week — and I closed it anyway. Not because the thesis broke. **Because the position was already too big.** I was carrying two older NOW lots at a combined **17% of the book**; the Aug 26 ticket was an add on top of that. It paid 2.7R in a day, and taking it off was the cheapest way to get the position back to a size I could be wrong about.

**That is the cost.** Not a loss — a give-up. I will never know what that add would have been worth, and I traded that number for the right to still be comfortable if software rolls over next week.

## Focus — what I'm watching

Next week is thin: back-to-school, desks refilling unevenly. **This is the whole book — five names; the other 48% is cash.** These are conditions, not forecasts.

**PLTR** — 155.37 from Aug 5, stop 153.00. **13.0R** after 24 days, 16% of the book across two lots.
*What matters:* the largest winner I have and the one I am least willing to give back.
*Plan:* adds only on a pullback to the 8/21 EMA. **I don't chase gaps**, and it sits 20% above my first entry.
*Wrong if:* it closes under 172.00, where the second lot's stop sits.

**NOW** — 119.83 and 125.43 from Aug 17–18, stops 116.00 and 125.00. **6.5R** and **3.6R**.
*What matters:* 17% of the book, carrying the software thesis by itself.
*Plan:* **no adds.** I took one off this week for exactly this reason.
*Wrong if:* it loses 125.00 — then the last working sector stopped working and the cash was the right call.

**ZETA** — 27.18 from Aug 9, stop 26.20. **3.4R** after 20 days.
*What matters:* it kept going up on Friday while almost nothing else did. That is the cleanest evidence I have that enterprise software is real leadership and not just the last thing left.
*Plan:* it holds itself; I add nothing into a tape making lower highs.
*Wrong if:* it closes under 26.20 — then software was crowding, not leadership.

**HOOD** — 96.72 and 104.10 from Aug 19–20, stops 92.00 and 100.00. **2.0R** and flat.
*What matters:* it is my most rate-sensitive position, and Friday was a rate shock. It fell 2.4% while software rose.
*Plan:* nothing while yields are rising. This is the position the week argued against.
*Wrong if:* the second lot loses 100.00. **I am watching this one to be wrong, not to be right.**

**FIG** — 28.64 from Aug 26, stop 27.70, three days old.
*What matters:* the only survivor of last week's six, and the live test of whether the dessert table holds.
*Plan:* it earns an add only if it clears 29.96 on a day the S&P is not printing a lower high.
*Wrong if:* it closes under 27.70. Then nothing from last week's six made it, and that is its own answer.

⚠️ **None of this is a recommendation and none of it has a target on it.** They are conditions. If a condition fails I was early — a different mistake from being wrong, and the one I would rather make.

**NOW (add) +2.7R** · **FIG** still open · **CRCL −0.6R** · **TEM −0.0R** · **MRNA 0.0R** · **SAIL 0.0R**

Now look at what is actually paying: **PLTR 13.0R over 24 days. NOW 6.5R over 12 days. ZETA 3.4R over 20 days.** Every one of them was opened *before* the week I just spent a thousand words describing.

**Almost nothing that paid last week was decided last week.** That is usually true, and it is the least dramatic fact in this business.

## 🔓 墙外（免费预览）

### The leaders are having dessert

**What this is**

I'm a full-time swing trader in Japan. Seven years in, what decided my results was never which names I picked. It was how much I put on each one, and where I got out. That is why this letter is called How Much.

Every Sunday: the number that changed, what I did about it, and what it cost me. The losers stay in.

**What this letter is not**

Not alerts. Nothing here is timed for you to copy. I post my own trades because a method you can't see me actually run is just a claim.

Not predictions. I don't have opinions about where the market goes. I have measurements of what it's doing and what I'm risking while it does it.

Not advice, and not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. I don't know your account, your taxes, or how well you sleep. Measure your own water.

---

Software closed last week up **6.88%**. The Russell 2000 closed **down 0.75%**. Those are the same five sessions.

Then Friday happened. The Fed chair came out of Jackson Hole hawkish, the dollar and yields ripped, and the indexes faded a morning rally to close near the lows. The S&P gave up 0.25%. The Russell gave up **1.39%** — and lost its 8-, 21- and 50-day averages in a single session.

Software was the only thing still working.

That is what a party looks like once it has started to end. The room empties, and the people still at the table are on dessert. I spent the first half of the week buying dessert and the second half asking for the check. **I finished Friday with 48% of the book in cash.**

Below is what that cost.

## What changed

**Net advances went from 1,431 to −1,408.**

One reading is an anecdote, so here are both ends of the ladder:

| | last week |
|---|---:|
| **IGV** (software) | **+6.88%** |
| SPY | +0.77% |
| **RSP** (equal-weight S&P) | **−0.56%** |
| **IWM** (small caps) | **−0.75%** |

**Read it from both ends.** A narrow slice of software had an excellent week; most of the market went quietly backwards. The S&P and its own equal-weight twin split by **1.33 points in five sessions** — the whole story in two numbers.

**I keep four breadth readings and I check all four, including the ones that disagree with me.** This week none of them disagreed: share of names above the 50-day sat in the 3rd percentile of 90 days, 10-day net advances in the 0th, the McClellan oscillator at −23, and new lows outnumbering new highs.

**But the tell of the week was not the index. It was NVDA.** The company delivered a genuinely strong report, gapped up on heavy volume, filled the gap, and closed lower. Marvell beat *and* raised and finished −10.3%. The cybersecurity names gapped up on good numbers and reversed into overhead supply. **When a tape stops paying for good news, the character has changed** — that is worth more than any breadth number I just showed you.

**So here is what I actually did, in two halves.**

**First half: I bought the leaders of the leading themes** — six tickets, all software and enterprise. I gave each one to three days to tell me something.

**Only two answered.**

| opened | | held | result |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Aug 24 | **TEM** — 67.25, stop 65.20 | 3 days | **−0.0R** |
| Aug 24 | **MRNA** — 151.70, stop 140.00 | 1 day | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **NOW** (add) — 132.89, stop 130.00 | 1 day | **+2.7R** |
| Aug 26 | **FIG** — 28.64, stop 27.70 | still on | +0.2R |
| Aug 26 | **SAIL** — 20.11, stop raised to 21.11 | 3 days | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **CRCL** — 94.47, stop 91.40 | 1 day | **−0.6R** |

**Second half: I took the check.** Five of the six came off within three days, and cash went from roughly 42% to **48.15%**.

**The one that worked is the one I want to explain, because it is the whole point of this letter.** NOW was my best answer of the week — and I closed it anyway. Not because the thesis broke. **Because the position was already too big.** I was carrying two older NOW lots at a combined **17% of the book**; the Aug 26 ticket was an add on top of that. It paid 2.7R in a day, and taking it off was the cheapest way to get the position back to a size I could be wrong about.

**That is the cost.** Not a loss — a give-up. I will never know what that add would have been worth, and I traded that number for the right to still be comfortable if software rolls over next week.

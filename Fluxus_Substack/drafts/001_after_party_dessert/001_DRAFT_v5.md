## 🔓 墙外（免费预览）

### The leaders are having dessert

Software closed last week up **6.88%**. The Russell 2000 closed **down 0.75%**. Those are the same five sessions.

Then Friday happened. The Fed chair came out of Jackson Hole hawkish, the dollar and yields ripped, and the indexes faded a morning rally to close near the lows. The S&P gave up 0.25%. The Russell gave up **1.39%** — and lost its 8-, 21- and 50-day averages in a single session.

Software was the only thing still working.

That is what a party looks like once it has started to end. The room empties, and the people still at the table are on dessert. I spent the first half of the week buying dessert and the second half asking for the check. **I finished Friday with 48% of the book in cash.**

Below is what that cost.

## 🔒 墙内

## What changed

**Net advances went from 1,431 to −1,408.**

One reading is an anecdote, so here is the whole ladder:

| | last week |
|---|---:|
| **IGV** (software) | **+6.88%** |
| XLK (tech) | +3.13% |
| QQQ | +1.43% |
| SPY | +0.77% |
| DIA | +0.26% |
| **RSP** (equal-weight S&P) | **−0.56%** |
| MDY (mid caps) | −0.56% |
| **IWM** (small caps) | **−0.75%** |

**Read it from both ends.** The top is a narrow slice of software having an excellent week. The bottom is most of the market going quietly backwards. The gap between the S&P and its own equal-weight twin — **1.33 points in five sessions** — is the whole story in two numbers.

**I keep four breadth readings and I check all four, including the ones that disagree with me.** This week none of them disagreed: share of names above the 50-day sat in the 3rd percentile of 90 days, 10-day net advances in the 0th, the McClellan oscillator at −23, and new lows outnumbering new highs.

⚠️ **That fourth one only agrees because we found a bug in it this weekend.** Our high/low count had been including SPACs — **58 of the 66 names it called 52-week highs were shell companies.** On the standard definition it is 8 highs against 15 lows. I had that reading backwards twice before I had it right, and I would rather tell you that than quietly publish the version that agrees with me.

**But the tell of the week was not the index. It was NVDA.** The company delivered a genuinely strong report, gapped up on heavy volume, filled the gap, and closed lower. Marvell beat *and* raised and finished −10.3%. The cybersecurity names gapped up on good numbers and reversed into overhead supply. **When a tape stops paying for good news, the character has changed** — that is worth more than any breadth number I just showed you.

**So here is what I actually did, in two halves.**

**First half: I bought the leaders of the leading themes** — six tickets, all software and enterprise. I gave each one to three days to tell me something.

**Only two answered.**

| opened | | held | result |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Aug 24 | **TEM** — 67.25, stop 65.20 | 3 days | **−0.0R** |
| Aug 24 | **MRNA** — 151.70, stop 140.00 | 1 day | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **NOW** (add) — 132.89, stop 130.00 | 1 day | **+2.7R** |
| Aug 26 | **FIG** — 28.64, stop 27.70 | still on | +0.2R |
| Aug 26 | **SAIL** — 20.11, stop raised to 21.11 | 3 days | **0.0R** |
| Aug 26 | **CRCL** — 94.47, stop 91.40 | 1 day | **−0.6R** |

**Second half: I took the check.** Four of the six came off the table within three days, and I raised cash from roughly 42% to **48.15%**.

**The one that worked is the one I want to explain, because it is the whole point of this letter.** NOW was my best answer of the week — and I closed it anyway. Not because the thesis broke. **Because the position was already too big.** I was carrying two older NOW lots at a combined **17% of the book**; the Aug 26 ticket was an add on top of that. It paid 2.7R in a day, and taking it off was the cheapest way to get the position back to a size I could be wrong about.

**That is the cost.** Not a loss — a give-up. I will never know what that add would have been worth, and I traded that number for the right to still be comfortable if software rolls over next week.

## Focus — what I'm watching

Next week is thin — back-to-school, desks refilling unevenly. Five names from the only four places anything still works: **software and cyber, semis and memory, agriculture, mega caps.** None is a position yet.

**CRM** — 256.00. Ripped 22.5% on the week on earnings and the Anthropic deal.
*What matters:* the cleanest breakout in the leading group — and **41% above its 50-day**, which is the problem.
*Plan:* **no chasing.** It is only interesting back at the 21-day EMA near 206.
*Wrong if:* it fails that EMA on the first test. A gap that fails its first pullback was distribution.

**RNG** — 69.38, **1.5% off its 52-week high**, up 3.4% on the week.
*What matters:* CRM's setup without the extension — base-on-base, twenty straight beat-and-raises.
*Plan:* it earns attention on a clean move through the 52-week high **on a day the S&P is not printing a lower high**.
*Wrong if:* it loses the 21-day at 62.88 — then even the un-extended software names are being sold.

**NVDA** — 217.55, sitting **on its 21-day EMA at 215.48**.
*What matters:* the whole market's question in one chart. A great report got sold on day two, and now it is standing on the only line that matters.
*Plan:* nothing until it resolves. **When good news can't lift price, believe the price.**
*Wrong if:* it closes under 215 — which I'd read as confirming the cash was right, not as a dip to buy.

**MU** — 932.86, also on its 21-day, and still **26% below its 52-week high**.
*What matters:* the laggard test. Semis moved from Lagging to Improving; if that's real it shows up here first.
*Plan:* it has to hold the 21-day *and* the group has to stop lagging. Both, not either.
*Wrong if:* it breaks the 21-day. Then "Improving" was a label, not a change.

**BG** — 115.48, up 4.8% on the week.
*What matters:* agriculture is still **Weakening** — the theme fell 1.35% on the week. BG rose anyway. That gap is the only reason it is here.
*Plan:* I do nothing until the theme itself turns. **One name moving inside a weakening group is a coincidence until it isn't.**
*Wrong if:* the group keeps sliding and BG rejoins it. Then it was a bounce, not a turn.

⚠️ **None of this is a recommendation and none of it has a target on it.** They are conditions. If a condition fails I was early — a different mistake from being wrong, and the one I would rather make.

# How I Caught a 176% Move in $MRNA

### *Three filters, five rules, and exactly how much: 0.25% for 23R*

---

**Aug 14.** I bought $MRNA at 62.72, stop at 60, five percent of the account.

**Aug 18.** The tape had been rotting for three sessions and the position had gone nowhere for three days, so I cut half, flat.

**Aug 19, pre-market.** The phase III data hit.

**Aug 19.** Opened up 84%, closed up **176.97%**. I was out ninety minutes after the open.

Those are the facts. The review has one point: **in the twelve days before that headline, nothing I did right had anything to do with it.**

You will leave with three things: **sector strength picks which one, tightness filters the trade, and risk determines the size.**

**I never ask will it go up.** I ask who is being bought, and then I hunt the next big elephant trade with tight risk.

## ① Sector — why not semis

〔📷 1 ▶ 板块三线图〕

Through the first half of August the answer to *who is being bought* was not semis, **which I owned and kept owning while they didn't work.** $MU ran 4% on the 17th and I felt smart for a day; on the 18th it took all of it back. The group hadn't printed a green number all month while software had the best excess in the market — and my book was already in software, because that rotation happened weeks earlier.

**Sector RS isn't sentiment — it's the footprint of Institutional algo monkeys**, and mine had been walking out for two weeks while I watched.

On any Thursday four hundred setups look valid, and sector strength earns its keep exactly when the index is weak — when everything is going up, outperformance means nothing.

Put your holdings' sector next to the index tonight and ask why you still hold the one that's behind. I couldn't answer that for $MU. That was the answer.

## ② The pool, and the one that surfaced

To qualify a stock needs two million shares a day, price above the 50-day, three-month RS in the top 20%. **That's the liquid leader definition, not mine, and it only tells you which water to fish.**

When to fish is my rule. I call it **surfacing** — a fish breaks the surface and you can't tell if it came up for air or is about to jump. They look the same. So the scanner doesn't judge, it hands me whatever surfaced today. All five, same day:

1. A 20-day high
2. A 4% up day in there
3. Above the 20-day
4. Three-month RS over 80
5. **RS self-percentile near 100** — today is its strongest day in twenty

〔📷 2 ▶ RS 折线 + 80 门槛〕

The fifth one is the scanner. The rest are qualifiers.

**RS isn't "went up a lot," it's "beat the market"** — a stock that falls less on a red day has rising RS, which is why it turns green while the chart still looks bad. The self-percentile compares it to nobody but itself: sort its own last twenty readings, see where today lands. A name crawling off the floor might sit at 60, nowhere near a leaderboard, **but self-percentile 100 means its character just changed and the rankings haven't noticed. That gap is where I get paid.**

**I bought intraday on the 14th and the scanner listed it that night. The machine confirmed me, it didn't tell me.**

*The timing standard I took from Jeff Sun's $KC breakdown — the list hands you the name before it moves, not after. What I didn't take is his holding period: he sits in a name for a month, my winners average ten days.*

## ③ The chart

Every average compressed into one band under price — the two-week money, the two-month money and the ten-month money all sitting at the same cost, and once price is above all of them, everyone underneath is carrying you.

Highest of the 10/20/50 minus the lowest, over price. **$MRNA went 25% to 4.1% in six weeks, six times tighter against itself.**

〔📷 3 ▶ 束宽五柱〕

My rule is four words: **no tightness, no trade.**

Then there's $60 — old resistance, a small handover, one big green bar through it. **Above 60 it's a trade, below 60 it's nothing.** The same level works twice for an unmystical reason:

> **The chart has no memory. The people who bought at that price do.**

〔📷 4 ▶ K 线 zoom〕

**Where the target came from:** COVID took this from the twenties to 120 with almost nobody trading on the way. Nobody accumulated in there, so nobody needs convincing to let price back through. **Empty up, empty back. Target 120, and quickly, or I'm wrong.**

Why the 19th? Nobody knows. What I knew was that 60 was the line, and I'd watched this shape resolve the same way too many times to ignore it.

## How much

**Two buys on Aug 14. Same stock, same day, same 5% — one cost thirteen times the other.**

**Trade 1, the structure:** in 62.72, stop 60, $2.72 a share, account risk **0.217%**.
**Trade 2, the VWAP add:** stop 20 cents under VWAP, account risk **0.016%**.

〔📷 5 ▶ 13.6× 对比〕

Same size, thirteen times the cost, and the difference was never conviction:

> **Position size is not risk. Stop distance is risk.**
> **Size = risk budget ÷ stop distance**

0.25% ÷ 4.34% ≈ 5%. **I didn't decide on 5%, I arrived at it.** The stop sits close because the structure is tight, not because I'm brave — and it runs in reverse too: **a sloppy chart with nowhere to put a stop gets a tiny position automatically. I never need discipline to pass on garbage. The arithmetic passes for me.**

## What I cut, and the once I added

**Aug 18.** Three sessions of decay — 63% of the market above its 20-day when I bought, under half by then — and the position had done nothing for three days. **My losers get three days. The rule came due and I paid it**, selling the add back at entry.

〔📷 6 ▶ 宽度三柱〕

**Two rules fired at once and neither knew a readout was twelve hours out.** That's not a flaw in the rules. That's what rules are.

**Aug 19, pre-market.** I trade from Asia, so the first leg happened while I was at breakfast. Price through 100, first selling into it, I sold half — **still with no idea what happened** — then did the least romantic thing in trading and asked an AI what the news was.

While it worked I pulled the long chart. Above 100 only history is left: **120 is old support turned resistance and the anchor off the all-time high lands at 126. Before I knew anything about oncology, I knew where the sellers lived.**

Biggest news this company's had since COVID. **So I bought it back pre-market**, and by the open I had 10% on at a lower cost than I started with.

> **When you don't know what happened, take risk off. When you know, put it back on. You decide in size, not in opinions.**

**First minute of the open, RVOL exploded** — not big volume, volume its own first minute has never seen. Above the one-minute opening range the buyers own the floor. **That open was a real entry for whoever wasn't in yet.** For me the same bars were the referee for selling on the way up.

〔📷 7 ▶ 一分钟图〕

**Ninety minutes in, flat.** The trade was still technically perfect when I left, which is exactly when to leave. It touched nearly 200 that afternoon and I was asleep.

Every cut cost me money in hindsight and **I'd make every one again** — the version of me that skips them doesn't survive the story where the readout goes the other way. I held it overnight, and **gaps don't check which direction you were hoping for.**

## The account

**R is what a trade is allowed to cost me, fixed before I click: 0.25%.** Not more when I really like it. There's no *really like it* line in the formula.

**R turns dollars into method** — the money never enters the division, so every decision happens inside the process instead of inside the money. **And R turns method into pain**: 0.25% is how much this one trade is allowed to hurt. **I set it while calm, so when the frightened version of me sits down there's nothing left for him to press.**

**Once the pain is bounded you stop needing more signal.** People hunt confirmation because they never drew a line around the uncertainty.

$MRNA that day: **+176.97%** · What I risked: **0.25%, one R** · What came back: **23R** · The account: **+17%**

The month after you post a number like that is usually the month you hand some back. Curse of showing off. I'll pay it.

**The luck was the data landing on day five of my hold.** I didn't predict a cancer-vaccine headline. What wasn't luck: the group I was already standing in, five rules that surfaced it, the line at 60, both stops, and the size I cut on the 18th.

**0.25% is what I paid to be in the room when the luck walked in.**

# How I Caught a 176% Move in $MRNA

### *The exact 3 filters, the 5 scanner rules, and exactly how much: 0.25% for 23R*

I cut my size the day before it went up 176%.

That sentence is the honest version of this trade, and I'd rather lead with it than bury it. I bought $MRNA on August 14th. The Merck data hit after the close on the 18th. In between, the market got ugly, the position went nowhere for three sessions, and I did what my rules told me to do, which was take half of it off.

Then it opened up 84% and ran to +176.97% on the day.

So this is not a story about conviction. I want to walk through what actually happened, in the order it actually happened, because the order is the whole thing. By the time $MRNA showed up as a candidate, most of the decisions had already been made — and none of them were about biotech.

## Three filters, in this order

Everything below runs top-down. Sector first, scanner second, chart third. If a name fails the first filter I never see it, which is the point.

| | What it asks | What it uses | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Between sectors** | Where is the money going? | Relative strength across 196 groups | **Before $MRNA existed to me** |
| **2. Inside the sector** | Who leads it? | Five scanner rules | Around Aug 12 |
| **3. This chart** | Can I act, and where does the risk go? | Structure — plus having seen this shape a hundred times | Aug 14 |

The first two are arithmetic. A machine runs them every night. The third one is half arithmetic and half something I can't hand you, and I'll be straight about which half is which when we get there.

## Filter 1 — Between sectors: why not semis

Through the first half of August the market was grinding. Not selling off, not going anywhere. Leadership was rotating slowly. And on the 13th and 14th, what I was actually watching was $MU — whether it could keep going.

It couldn't. Every push failed.

Here's the part most write-ups leave out: **I owned semis. It wasn't working.**

| $MU | Aug 14 | Aug 17 | Aug 18 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close | 971.66 | ~1011.8 | **940.76** |
| Day | +2.3% | **+4.1%** | **−7.0%** |

August 17th looked like the breakout continuing. August 18th gave all of it back and then some. I wasn't standing outside the sector calling it weak. I was inside it, losing money in it.

And that's what the group data had been saying for two weeks. Three-month excess return, archived nightly, not picked after the fact:

| Group | Aug 7 | Aug 12 | **Aug 14** | Aug 17 | Aug 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Software** | +0.078 | +0.208 | **+0.216** | +0.183 | +0.172 |
| **Biotech** | +0.118 | +0.131 | **+0.153** | **+0.202** | — |
| Genomics | 0.000 | +0.130 | +0.103 | +0.135 | **+0.312** |
| **Semiconductors** | **−0.103** | **−0.140** | **−0.118** | **−0.092** | **−0.192** |

Semis never printed a positive number all month. Software was the strongest group in the market on the day I bought. My existing book — $PLTR, $NOW, $ZETA, $RBRK — was already sitting in it, because that rotation happened weeks earlier.

This is the filter nobody writes about. Everyone writes about what a setup looks like. Almost nobody writes about the fact that on any given Thursday there are four hundred valid-looking setups, and something has to decide which one you're allowed to spend risk on.

For me that decision is sector relative strength — and it matters most **when the index is weak.** When everything's ripping, outperformance tells you nothing. When the index is sliding and one group is still beating it, that spread is real. August 14th through 18th the index fell three days straight. That's exactly when this reading is cleanest.

Then, inside the strong group, I want the higher-volatility name. $XBI moves 2.71% a day. $MRNA moved 5.87%. Same stop distance, twice the payoff. **That's what "better risk/reward" means in practice — not a feeling, a ratio.**

## Filter 2 — Inside the sector: the five rules

My liquid-leader scan wants all five:

1. Three-month relative strength **above 80**
2. A **20-day high**
3. RS self-percentile **near 100** — today is its strongest reading of the last twenty days
4. A **4% up day** in there
5. Price **above the 20-day**

Rule 3 is the one that matters and the one that needs explaining.

**Relative strength isn't "went up a lot." It's "went up more than the market."** A stock that falls less in a down tape has rising RS. That's why it beats price as a signal — it turns green while the chart still looks bad.

I use two versions of it, and they answer different questions:

- **RS level** — over three months, how far ahead of the market is it? *Compared to everything else.*
- **RS self-percentile** — where does today's RS rank inside **its own** last twenty days?

The second one is the early bell. A stock climbing off the floor might still have an RS level of 60 — nowhere near the leaderboard. But if its self-percentile is 100, **its character has already changed and the rankings just haven't caught up.**

$MRNA's three-month RS over eight sessions, archived each night:

> **56 → 63 → 83 → 81 → 82 → 82 → 86 → 88**

Its RS self-percentile over the same stretch went from 48 to 100.

One footnote on honesty: **I bought on the 14th, and the scan listed it that night.** I saw it on the list the next morning. The machine confirmed me; it didn't tell me. I'd rather say that than pretend the software was driving.

> *I took the timing idea from Jeff Sun's $KC breakdown — the list has to hand you the name before it moves, not after. What I didn't take is his holding period. He can sit in something for a month; my winners average ten days. Same logic, different people, different rules.*

## Filter 3 — The chart: what a launchpad is, and how tight is tight

The structure has a name: **launchpad**, Ross Haber's term. I didn't learn it from him and then go buy — but his explanation and mine are the same one. **All the moving averages compress into a single band underneath the price. Every timeframe's holders now sit at roughly the same cost. And once price is above all of them, everyone underneath you is waiting to be lifted.**

Ten, twenty, fifty — squeezed together, then they fan out. **What you're buying is the compression. The expansion is what you're waiting for.**

Now — "compressed" is a thing you can see, which means it's a thing you can measure. Take the highest of the 10/20/50-day averages, subtract the lowest, divide by price. That's the width of the band.

| | MA10 | MA20 | MA50 | Band | % of price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jul 15 | 73.52 | 68.19 | 56.47 | $17.05 | **25.0%** |
| Jul 31 | 56.70 | 63.74 | 58.41 | $7.05 | **12.9%** |
| Aug 6 | 55.50 | 59.26 | 59.06 | $3.76 | **7.0%** |
| Aug 11 | 56.90 | 58.10 | 59.81 | $2.91 | **4.8%** |
| **Aug 13** | 58.39 | 57.90 | 60.52 | **$2.62** | **4.1%** |

**Six times tighter in six weeks.** Not "looks tight" — tight *relative to itself*, which is the only comparison that means anything.

Take that number. It's the most portable thing in this letter. You can put it in a screener tonight.

### The eight sessions before I bought

- **Jul 31** — earnings. Five days of chop underneath.
- **Aug 7** — engulfs the 6th, **+10%, reclaims the 21 EMA.** After that, price never goes back down. A range is born.
- **Aug 10** — reclaims the 50-day.
- **Aug 11** — holds above 60.
- **Aug 12** — **first close above the 20-day. Structure flips long.** Same day: 20-day high, +5%. That's when it entered my field of view properly.
- **Aug 13** — a doji. **The first real fight between buyers and sellers.**
- **Aug 14** — the pullback into that long structure. **I buy.**

Why was there a fight on the 13th? Because of a heavy red bar on July 16th. The volume wasn't big, but below the 20-day the long side loses its engine and leaves. So the 13th was a test of whether any of those sellers were still sitting there.

They weren't.

And $60 is the line the whole thing hangs on. Prior resistance, then a small handover, then one large green bar straight through it. **Above 60 this trade is interesting. Below 60 it isn't a trade.** The same level works twice for the same reason:

> **Price has memory.**

### Why this one, specifically

Two things about $MRNA itself.

**One: it had been based for a year.** Longer in base, higher in space. The longer the spring compresses, the more there is on the other side.

And it has an unusual feature. From COVID it went from twenty-something to 120 in a few months — six times, almost no trading on the way. That's an imbalance. **On a chart it's a fast zone: a stretch of price where almost nobody accumulated, so almost nobody has to be convinced to let it through.** When it broke down it fell through the same zone just as fast, in the other direction.

Which gives me something most setups don't hand you: **a target and a speed.** If this base resolves, there's a high probability it retraces that zone quickly. That's 120.

> **I'm not holding $MRNA at 140 waiting for it to see 400 again. That's not the trade I'm in. I want that one specific fast stretch, and I want it quickly.**

**Two: this stock's behavior is unusually regular.** Every bottom reversal it makes runs the same way — engulf, turn, structure flips long, go. Pull back to the 20-day, reclaim, go. Over and over. There's always a bid underneath, which is what accumulation looks like from outside. And every breakout gets a pullback, because that's the cheaper price being taken.

**This is the part I can't hand you.** The band width is arithmetic. The RS is arithmetic. Knowing what this *kind* of chart tends to do next is just having seen a lot of them. **The pattern becomes muscle memory; the measurable edge is what keeps you solvent while you use it.** Those are two different jobs and you need both.

What I could *not* tell you is why it chose the 19th to go. Nobody could. What I could tell you is that 60 was the line, and that these reversals in this name have a leg in them.

## How much

This is the part almost nobody publishes, so here it is completely.

I bought twice on August 14th. **Both positions were 5% of the account. One of them cost thirteen times more than the other.**

| | Structural entry | The VWAP add |
|---|---|---|
| Fill | **62.72** | above VWAP, same day |
| Stop | **60** — under the line | **20 cents** under VWAP |
| Risk per share | **$2.72** (4.34% of price) | **$0.20** (0.32%) |
| Size | **5%** | **5%** |
| **Account risk** | **0.217%** | **0.016%** |

Same size. Thirteen-point-six times the cost.

The first one buys the structure, so the stop has to sit under the line that would prove the structure wrong. That's $2.72 a share and there's no negotiating it. The second one buys order in that moment — above VWAP, in the direction of the day — and if it's wrong I know within minutes. So the same 5% costs almost nothing.

**Position size is not risk. Stop distance is risk.**

There's a chain running through this whole letter and it's three links long:

> **Band width 4.5% → stop sits 0.73 ATR away → 5% of the account costs 0.217%.**

The stop is close because the structure is tight. Not because I'm timid. **A messy chart with no logical stop mechanically gets a tiny position — I don't have to summon discipline to pass on it. The arithmetic passes for me.**

Total on August 14th: 10% of the account, 0.233% at risk.

## Four times I made it smaller

**One — August 14th.** My own regime read said the environment was fine. Full size, normal adds. I put on 5%.

**Two — August 18th.** The tape deteriorated three sessions running. This isn't a feeling; it's a count:

| | **Aug 14** | Aug 17 | **Aug 18** |
|---|---|---|---|
| % of stocks above their 20-day | 63.3% | 56.2% | **49.5%** |
| Stocks up 4% / down 4% | 511 / 434 | 349 / 676 | **320 / 812** |
| Advancers / decliners | 2795 / 2521 | 1908 / 3491 | **2051 / 3314** |

On the day I bought, 63% of the market was above its 20-day line. Three days later, under half.

And the position itself had done nothing for three sessions — 63.32, then 64.46, then 62.96. **My losers get three days. That's a written rule, and it had come due.**

So I sold the add back out around my entry price, flat, no loss. I'd rebuild when the market improved.

Two rules fired at once. Neither of them knew a phase III readout was coming that night.

**Three — August 19th, pre-market.** Price came through 100 and I saw the first selling into it. I sold half. **I still had no idea what the news was.**

**Four — August 19th, ninety minutes after the open.** Out.

Four decisions to hold less. Every one of them cost me money in hindsight. **I'd make all four again, because the version of me that doesn't make them is also the version that doesn't survive the trades where the news goes the other way.** I held that position over a gap. Gaps have two directions.

## The morning of the 19th

**I trade from Asia. The whole first leg happened without me.**

7am New York is 6am here. I finished eating, came back to the desk, and there it was — one clean break higher on the hourly.

I didn't know what had happened. So I did the only sensible thing with a position that has gone somewhere unexplained: **I took half off into the first selling I saw, just over 100.**

Then I went and asked an AI what the news was and how big it was.

While that ran, I pulled up the long-term chart. Above 100 we were through a 52-week high, so the only levels left are historical. **120 is old support-turned-resistance. An anchor drawn from the all-time high lands around 126.** So before I knew anything about oncology, I knew where I'd likely meet sellers.

It was 7:10am New York. **Two and a half hours to the open.** Volume hadn't picked up. There was no real supply in that window.

> **My guess was that most traders simply weren't at their desks yet.**

The answer came back: the biggest news this company has had since COVID.

**So I bought it back, pre-market, when the moving averages caught up and it pushed through again.** Trimmed at 120 and 128 on the way. By the open I had 10% on at a lower average cost than I'd started with.

Then I just followed it up and sold into it — 124, 128, and the last piece at 150. **Ninety minutes after the open I was flat.**

It went to nearly 200 after hours. I didn't get that.

## For anyone who wasn't already in

One more thing from that morning, and this part isn't about my position at all — **it's the entry that was available to someone who hadn't been holding it.** I watched it. For me the same bars were a scale-out signal, not an entry.

I watched the one-minute, not the five.

**The first minute of the open printed 140k shares.** Relative volume was enormous. From there I used the one-minute opening range as the referee: **above it, buyers have the floor; below it, sellers do.** That's the whole rule.

VWAP did the rest. It reclaimed VWAP on the first five-minute bar, and above VWAP you go with it. **The entry is right there, and the stop goes just under the bar that reclaimed it.** That's about $4 — 3.8%.

**That $4 is not my risk on this trade.** Mine was $2.72 on the 14th and 20 cents on the add. Three different numbers, two different days, and one of them belongs to a reader, not to me. I'm separating them because writing them as one number would be a lie by tidiness.

## The account

Before the number, the unit.

**R is what a trade is allowed to cost me, decided before I enter. For me, 1R is 0.25% of the account.** Fixed. Not "more when I like it."

Once R is fixed, size stops being a decision and becomes division: **size = risk budget ÷ stop distance.** Close stop, bigger position. Wide stop, smaller position, automatically.

And afterwards I count in R, not dollars. **Dollars tell you how big my account is, which is none of your business and no use to you. R tells you the shape of the method, which is the part you can take.**

| | |
|---|---|
| $MRNA on the day | **+176.97%** (62.96 → 174.38) |
| What I risked | **0.25% — 1R** |
| What came back | **23R** |
| The account that day | **+17%** |

You can check the 23R: in at 62.72, stop at 60, so $2.72 a share. 62.72 + 23 × 2.72 = $125.30 — right in the middle of where I actually sold.

**Merck was luck.** I didn't predict a cancer-vaccine headline and I've said I couldn't tell you why the 19th. What wasn't luck: the sector I was standing in, the five rules that surfaced it, the line at 60, the two stops, and the size I cut on the 18th.

**0.25% is what I paid to be in the room when the luck showed up.**

## On not eating the whole fish

I left the last piece on the table and I want to be clear that this was on purpose.

**Don't try to eat the head or the tail. Take the part that belongs to you.** Get out completely when you're high, even when the trade is technically still perfectly valid. Especially then.

And some of it is simply where I live. **I trade from Asia. I was never going to sit up until 5am to catch that final leg.**

> **Between health and wealth — I already had the wealth. So I took the health.**

---

The news is never in the chart.

The price of being there is.

That's the only question I ask before anything I buy. Not *will this go up.* **How much am I paying to find out.**

Here's the thing nobody writes about. **On any Thursday there are four hundred setups that look valid. Something has to decide which one gets your risk.** Mine is sector relative strength — and it earns its keep precisely **when the index is weak.** When everything's ripping, outperformance means nothing. And here's why I trust those numbers with actual money: **nobody's opinion is in them.** Funds get forced by their own risk rules to dump weakness and chase strength. They don't get a vote. **Sector RS isn't sentiment — it's the footprint of machines that don't have any.**

Do this tonight, it takes five minutes: your holdings' sector against the index, same window. The one that's behind — **why are you still in it?** I couldn't answer that for $MU. That's the point.

### ② The pool, and the one that surfaced

My pool needs three things: two million shares a day, above the 50-day, three-month RS top 20%. **That's the liquid leader definition — not mine — and it only tells you which water to fish. It says nothing about when.**

*When* is my rule. I call it **surfacing** — the second a fish breaks the surface, you can't tell if it came up for air or is about to jump. They look identical. So the scanner doesn't judge. It just hands me whatever broke the surface today. All five, same day:

1. A 20-day high
2. A 4% up day in there
3. Above the 20-day
4. Three-month RS over 80
5. **RS self-percentile near 100** — today is its strongest day in twenty

Number five is the whole scanner. The rest is plumbing.

**RS isn't "went up a lot." It's "beat the market."** A stock that falls less on a red day has rising RS — it turns green while the chart still looks like garbage. And the self-percentile doesn't care about any other stock on earth: **sort its own last twenty readings, see where today lands.** That's the entire calculation. A name crawling off the floor might have an RS of 60 — nowhere near any leaderboard — but self-percentile at 100 means **its character just changed and the rankings haven't noticed yet.**

That gap — changed but not yet noticed — is where I get paid.

### ③ The chart

The structure's called a **launchpad**: every average compressed into one narrow band under price. Which means every timeframe's holders — the two-week money, the two-month money, the ten-month money — all sitting at roughly the same cost. Price above all of them? **Everyone underneath is carrying you.**

And compression is visible, so it's measurable. Highest of the 10/20/50, minus the lowest, over price. **$MRNA: 25% to 4.1% in six weeks. Six times tighter — against itself**, the only opponent that counts.

〔📷 6 ▶ 束宽五柱〕

My rule has four words: **no tightness, no trade.**

Then there's $60 — the nail this whole thing hangs on. Old resistance, a small handover, one big green bar straight through. **Above 60 it's a trade. Below 60 it isn't anything.** Same level, works twice, and the reason isn't mystical:

> **The chart has no memory. The people who bought at that price do.**

## How much

Almost nobody publishes this part. Which is insane, because it's the only part that's actually yours.

**I bought twice on Aug 14. Same day, same stock, same 5%. One position cost thirteen times the other.**

**Trade 1 — the structure.** In 62.72, stop 60. $2.72 a share. Account risk **0.217%**.
**Trade 2 — the VWAP add.** Stop 20 cents under VWAP. Account risk **0.016%**.

〔📷 10 ▶ 13.6× 对比〕

Same size. Thirteen-point-six times the cost. The difference was never conviction —

> **Position size is not risk. Stop distance is risk.**
>
> **Size = risk budget ÷ stop distance**

On the 14th: 0.25% ÷ 4.34% ≈ 5%. **I didn't decide on 5%. I arrived at it.** And the stop is close because the structure is tight, not because I'm brave. Run it backwards and it's even better: **a sloppy chart with nowhere to put a stop gets a tiny position automatically. I don't need discipline to pass on garbage. The arithmetic passes for me.**

Answer came back: biggest news this company's had since COVID. **So I bought it back — pre-market, when the averages caught up and it pushed through again.** By the open: 10% on, at a lower cost than I started with.

## The account

The unit first, because the unit is the product.

**R is what a trade is allowed to cost me, fixed before I click: 0.25% of the account.** Not "more when I really like it." There is no "really like it" line item.

**It turns dollars into method.** The money never enters the division. Every decision after that happens inside the process, not inside the money.

**And it turns method into pain.** Because 0.25% isn't really a risk parameter — it's a pain parameter. How much this one trade is allowed to hurt. **I set that number while calm, so when the frightened version of me finally sits down at the desk, there's nothing left for him to press.**

**Once the pain is bounded, you stop needing more signal.** People hunt confirmation not because confirmation works but because they never drew a line around the uncertainty — so the only thing left to buy is feeling a little more sure. Bound the pain. You won't miss the feeling.

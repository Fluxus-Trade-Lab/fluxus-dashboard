Everyone tells you what to buy. Almost nobody tells you how much — and how much is the part that decides your year.

You can be right about the name and finish flat, because you bought it like a lottery ticket and sized it like a mortgage. You can be right twice and hand it all back on the third, because you never wrote down where you'd be wrong. I've been both of those people, and the trades are in here with dates on them.

## The record, before anything else

331 closed trades this year. 132 of them made money, 18 went nowhere, and 181 lost.

The year works anyway, and here is the whole reason: **47 of those 331 made more than three times what I had at risk.** That's the job. Everything else is keeping the other 284 small enough that the 47 still count.

I can't tell you which 47 it will be. Nobody can. What I can do is set, in advance, what each one is allowed to cost me.

## The one number

My risk on a single idea is **0.25% of the account**, fixed before I click. Not more when I really like it — there is no *really like it* line in the formula.

Size is that number divided by the distance to my stop. Which means a clean chart with a tight stop gets a big position and a sloppy one gets a small position, automatically. I never have to summon discipline to pass on garbage. The arithmetic passes for me.

Every number in that paragraph is one you can recompute. There is nothing in the formula that requires me.

## Who's writing

Full-time discretionary swing trader in Japan, trading US hours from Asia. I started as a day trader and spent two years learning that being right at 10:04 and wrong at 10:11 pays the same as being wrong twice. I learned under a hedge fund manager who had been through every bear market since the 1980s, which is a long way of saying I learned defense before I learned anything fun.

The market isn't the world. It takes real-world inputs and then mostly ignores them. The crowd shows up after the move is already on the chart. The big funds don't show up at all — they sell whatever their risk limits tell them to sell, on a schedule, without an opinion, which makes them the calmest counterparty you will ever get. My only edge is being the one in the room who did the arithmetic before he felt anything.

I won't promise to be right. Accuracy isn't mine to control, and anybody who promises it is selling you something. What I control is that the arithmetic is finished before I click, and that you can see it afterwards.

---

**What this letter is**

A letter about one number. Every idea here arrives with its entry, its stop, and what I'm risking in percent — of the trade and of the account.

A record kept in public, losers included. My win rate is 39.9%. You will see the ones that didn't work, because a letter that only shows winners teaches the wrong job.

A method you can run without me. I measure. You decide.

**What this letter is not**

Not alerts. Everything here is written after the fact, with the arithmetic attached, so you can check it — not so you can copy it in the next four minutes. I post my own trades because a method you can't watch me run is just talk.

Not predictions. I don't have opinions about where the market goes. I have measurements of what it's doing and what I'm risking while it does it.

Not advice, and not a recommendation to buy or sell anything. I don't know your account, your taxes, or how well you sleep. Measure your own water.

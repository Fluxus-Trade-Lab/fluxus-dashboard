In June the account was down 11.1% from its high. Eight sessions, about an eighth of the year's work sitting red, and no way at the time to know whether it was eight sessions or the start of something worse.

That is the honest place to start a page about performance, because it's the part that decides whether a record is worth anything. Everybody's equity curve looks the same on the way up.

## The half year

| | |
|---|---|
| H1 2026 | **+90.5%** |
| SPY, same window | **+9.31%** |
| Max drawdown, daily mark-to-market | **–11.1%** |
| Closed trades | 331 |
| Average hold | 7.5 days |

I'd rather you read the third row than the first.

A 90% half-year with a 40% drawdown behind it is a different object than a 90% half-year with an 11% drawdown behind it — one is leverage and luck, the other is a process that stayed the same size while it was working. The return tells you what happened. **The drawdown tells you what it cost to be there for it, which is the only part you'd have had to live through.**

## What the number is made of

The 90.5% is not the interesting part. This is:

**39.9% of those 331 trades made money.** I was wrong three times out of five. The year works because the winners are **3.40×** the losers — not because I'm good at picking, but because I'm strict about how much a wrong one is allowed to cost.

**Forty-seven trades finished better than 3R.** Out of 331. Cut those forty-seven and the year goes flat. The right tail isn't a bonus on top of the performance — it *is* the performance, and everything else in the method exists to keep me solvent and in size until one shows up.

**One hundred and thirty trades ended with nothing but the stop firing.** Roughly one per business day. No story, no insight, no post about it. That's what the job actually looks like from the inside.

## Why I show it this way

Most performance pages in this business are a screenshot of a brokerage balance. A screenshot tells you a number. It doesn't tell you the drawdown, the hit rate, the payoff, how concentrated the wins were, or whether the whole thing was one lucky position.

I'd rather hand you the parts that let you decide it's repeatable — or decide it isn't. **Arrogant in the number, humble in the method:** the return is real and I'm not going to be coy about it, but the return is the *result*. The method is the product, and the method is the thing you can actually use.

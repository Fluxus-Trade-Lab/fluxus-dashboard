A chart that
argues

TradingView and a terminal are built for operating — the tools are the product. Counted on a live TradingView chart just now: 14 icons down the left, 12 across the top, 9 down the right before a single pixel of data. That is correct for someone who already knows what they are looking for. It is useless for the person you are handing a screenshot to.

Our charts have a different job: carry the argument. Same numbers, drawn so the conclusion is visible without being trusted.

FLUXUS — the gap is the subject

Same four series. On the left you must already know that RSP is the equal-weight proxy, and you must do the subtraction yourself. On the right the subtraction is the drawing — the wedge is the distance between the average stock and the index. Nothing was added; one relationship was promoted.

And it caught an error. The first version of this chart was captioned "the index is carried by a few names." Over these 30 sessions the equal-weight is +2.80% against the index's +0.04% — the average stock led. The narrowness is a one-day fact: today SPY +0.72% against RSP −0.17%. The honest reading is the decay: the lead peaked at +5.5 points on 07/29 and has halved in two sessions. A chart that carries its argument also has to be able to lose it.

Every price chart in the world ends at the last bar. This one continues one slot past it and leaves it empty. That empty slot is the whole posture: waiting for FTD is a position on a timeline, not an adjective.

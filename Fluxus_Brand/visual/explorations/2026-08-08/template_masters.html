FLUXUS Seven grammars —

Seven grammars,
one day

The same 2026-07-31 numbers, drawn seven ways — each borrowing the grammar of one founder of the field. Nothing here is a style exercise: each grammar makes a different thing easy to see, and the point of putting them side by side is to find out which question our page is actually asking. Each panel also names who is carrying that grammar today, and marks whether I actually went and looked.

01
Isotype
OTTO NEURATH · GERD ARNTZ · VIENNA, 1925–34
Quantity as repeated identical units, never as a stretched bar. A bigger number is more symbols, not a longer rectangle — so the reader counts instead of estimating. Arntz's pictograms are the direct ancestors of Aicher's Munich system.
Carrying it now Sonja Kuijpers (studio terp), The Pudding's unit pieces, and the whole "one dot = one person" school in news graphics. not visited
What it wins: "Leadership is broadening" stops being an adjective. Six marks are six names. This is the strongest answer we have to show the evidence. What it costs: it caps out — 133 marks in a row is unreadable, so big counts need a "1 mark = 10" convention, which quietly reintroduces estimation.
02
Minard's flow
CHARLES JOSEPH MINARD · PARIS, 1869
A single band whose width is the quantity, travelling along the path of the process. On the Russian campaign map the army's thinning is the casualty count — no second chart, no legend. Loss is visible as a shape.
Carrying it now Moritz Stefaner (Truth & Beauty) — dark ground, acid accent, terrain and flow work, rigorous and graphically strong. visited
What it wins: the propagation chain finally has a magnitude. It is not six lamps, it is a current that gets thinner at each link and dies at link three. What it costs: it implies the links are a physical pipeline. They are conditions, not plumbing.
03
Du Bois portraits
W.E.B. DU BOIS · PARIS EXPOSITION, 1900
Hand-drawn modernist abstraction, flat saturated colour on buff paper, forms bent to fit the page — decades before the Bauhaus. And every plate was an argument, made to change what a viewer believed. Proof that rigour and art are not a trade.
Carrying it now Mona Chalabi (hand-drawn, argumentative), and the annual #DuBoisChallenge recreation community. not visited
What it wins: it looks like nothing in finance, and it is still honest. This is the closest any of the seven gets to your own taste. What it costs: the palette is loud enough to break the anti-dopamine rule if used daily — this is a letter cover grammar, not an instrument grammar.
04
Bertin's matrix
JACQUES BERTIN · SÉMIOLOGIE GRAPHIQUE, 1967
The reorderable matrix: put every item against every criterion, then permute rows and columns until blocks appear. The structure is found by sorting, not by drawing. Bertin also ranks the visual variables — and hue is one of the weakest for ordered data, which is a direct challenge to how we currently encode response quality.
Carrying it now Moritz Stefaner again, and the Observable / Mike Bostock lineage where "sort until it speaks" is standard practice. visited
What it wins: it is testable. Sorted by rung, the damaged cohort forms a solid block in the bottom-right — narrowness as a shape, not a sentence. And it uses lightness, not hue, so it survives greyscale and colour blindness. What it costs: it is the least emotional object on this page. Nobody screenshots a matrix.
05
The transit diagram
HARRY BECK 1933 · MASSIMO VIGNELLI 1972
Lie about the geometry to tell the truth about the system. Only 45° and 90°, equal spacing between stops, distance discarded. Topology over geography — the reader needs the order of the stops, not the map.
Carrying it now Pentagram (Bierut's MTA work; the Vignelli Standards Manual reissue) and Jug Cerović's one-line metro systems. not visited
What it wins: a stranger reads it in three seconds with zero training, because they already know how to read this diagram. That is the whole brief. What it costs: equal spacing hides that link three is a far bigger jump than link one.
06
The coxcomb
FLORENCE NIGHTINGALE · LONDON, 1858
Polar wedges where area is the quantity, and two periods drawn in the same frame so the change is the picture. She built it to move a government, and it did. The founding case of a chart made to change a decision.
Carrying it now Federica Fragapane — organic, radial, unconventional forms that stay rigorous. site unreachable from here
What it wins: one month against today in a single form, and it is beautiful enough to be a cover. What it costs: area is genuinely hard to judge — Nightingale's own diagram overstates. For an instrument this is a real accuracy loss, not a style opinion.
07
Data humanism
GIORGIA LUPI · DEAR DATA 2015 · MANIFESTO 2017
A per-item glyph that carries several attributes at once, plus a key that reads like a personal alphabet. Warmth does not come from decoration — it comes from a visible author who chose what to measure. Which is the exact answer to whether "warm" can coexist with "zero decoration": it can, because they live in different layers.
Carrying it now Giorgia Lupi herself — Pentagram partner since 2019, "data as a visual language to tell stories and shape brand missions", first solo show at Gallerie d'Italia. visited · also Stefanie Posavec. not visited
What it wins: nineteen names become nineteen individuals instead of nineteen rows. And it is the only grammar here with a place for a human author. What it costs: every reader must learn the key first. That is fine for a letter, fatal at 6:30am.

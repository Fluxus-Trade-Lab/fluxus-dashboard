Light mode was measured on 2026-08-08 and passed: the two fills bottom out at ΔE 19.3 in greyscale, against 3.3 for the old four-colour scheme. That number is a property of the pair, so it survives the theme flip unchanged — and it is the wrong number to be reassured by.

Dark mode moves the two things the pair was measured against: the ground drops from L* 95 to L* 9, and --color-v2-off — the empty cell, the thing that makes rank = how many cells are filled legible — flips from a near-white #E6E2DB to a near-black #3A3733. The fills did not move with them. --color-took and --color-refused are not overridden under html.dark at all; they inherit the light values.

So the question here is not "are the two fills still separable" (they are). It is is a filled cell still separable from an empty one, and is any fill still separable from the ground it now sits on. Both are measured below, and one of them fails.

What these numbers are, and are not

Greyscale is the only load-bearing column. It is a true linear-luminance projection — after it, a* and b* are exactly zero, so greyscale ΔE is |ΔL*| and nothing else. It is also the condition this brand actually meets every time a page is screenshotted, pasted into a letter, or printed.

The colour-blindness columns are directional only. The Vienot-style matrices used here are not luminance-preserving, so they routinely read higher than normal vision — which cannot be true of a simulation: removing a channel can never add discriminability. Read them as "blue↔orange is a known dichromat-safe pair", never as magnitudes.

The rendered panels are illustration, not measurement. CSS filter:grayscale(1) uses the non-linear sRGB luma matrix, which is close to but not identical to the linear-light projection in the tables. The tables are the authority; the panels are there so the failure is something you can see rather than something you have to trust.

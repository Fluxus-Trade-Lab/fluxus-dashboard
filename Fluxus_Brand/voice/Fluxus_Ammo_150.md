# 弹药库 · 精选 150

*从 `Fluxus_Ammo_Library.md` 的原始堆里筛出。**按「什么行情发什么」排序**——建库按对手,用库按行情。*
*标注:【对手】【form】。⭐ = 我押注最重的。*

## 砍掉的标准(我实际执行的)

1. **没有画面的砍。**「信心是账户曲线的滞后指标」——像鸡汤,不像你。
2. **重复机制的只留一条。** 费雪「他没发明的叫止损」与牛顿「微积分是他发明的,止损不是」= 同一个包袱,留牛顿。
3. **拍 2 在说理的砍。** 一解释,节奏就死。
4. **用了烂梗的砍。**「这次不一样」是财经圈的谐音梗。
5. **⚠️ 开头单调的改写。** 原稿有 24 条四拍式都是「没人会因为⋯⋯就⋯⋯」开头。**保留结构,换开口**——否则连发三条就露公式。

**A1** ⭐⭐【牛顿】【反高潮】
> 1720 年,牛顿买了南海公司的股票。涨了一倍,他卖了,落袋七千镑。他觉得这个市场已经疯了,不能再待。
> 他是对的。
> 然后他眼看着他所有的朋友继续赚钱。
> 他又买回去了。这次亏了两万镑。

**E-4** ⭐⭐ LTCM
> In 1997 two of them won the Nobel Prize for how to price an option.
> In 1998 the fund they sat on lost four point six billion dollars in under four months.
> One year.
> The model wasn't wrong. The model just didn't have a line in it for *everybody runs at once*.

**E-5** ⭐ LTCM
> Twelve months between the Nobel Prize and the margin call.

**E-6** ⭐ LTCM
> Leverage doesn't magnify your returns. It translates "I was wrong" into "I'm out."

**E-7** ⭐⭐ Graham
> Between 1929 and 1932, Benjamin Graham lost seventy percent.
> His income was gone, so he took a book deal to cover the bills.
> The book was *Security Analysis*.
> The bible of value investing is a byproduct of a drawdown.

**E-8** ⭐ Graham
> He didn't write that book because he was winning. He wrote it because he wasn't.

**E-9** ⭐⭐ Keynes
> In May 1920 Keynes was wiped out trading currencies. Technically he was bankrupt. He stayed alive by borrowing from his friends.
> He apologized to each of them.
> In 1923 he started again with about a hundred and twenty-five thousand and doubled it in five years.
> He didn't get smarter. He was already the smartest man in the room. He just started living differently.

**E-10** ⭐ Keynes
> A bad currency trader and a great investor. Same brain, different game.

**E-11** ⭐⭐ 系列
> Newton. Fisher. Two Nobel laureates. Keynes. Graham.
> There isn't a stupid person on that list.
> There also isn't one who lost because he wasn't smart enough.
> They lost because intelligence doesn't govern this part. Position size does.

**E-12** ⭐ 系列
> The smartest minds our species has produced have all lost money in a market. That road's been walked. You don't have to walk it again to prove anything.

**E-13** ⭐⭐ Fisher
> On October 15th, 1929, the most famous economist in America stood up at a dinner in New York and said stock prices had reached what looked like a permanently high plateau.
> Nine days later it started coming apart.
> What everyone remembers is how stupid that sounds.
> What nobody remembers is that he was leveraged to the hilt. He wasn't forecasting. He was explaining his position.

**E-14** ⭐⭐ Fisher
> The most dangerous opinion you hold is the one you've already put money behind.

**E-15** ⭐ Fisher
> Fisher never took it back. He wrote essays explaining it was temporary. The market kept falling and he kept writing.
> He wrote for three years.
> After three years he stopped, because the money was gone.
> Admitting you're wrong is a job. If you won't do it, somebody does it for you.

**E-16** ⭐⭐ 过去的自己

**E-17** ⭐⭐ 过去的自己

**E-18** ⭐ 过去的自己

**E-20** ⭐⭐ 视角

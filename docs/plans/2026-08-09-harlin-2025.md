# 2025: the CDSS-to-trading transition, in his own words

Mined from @TailThatWagsDog Feb–Dec 2025. This is the year the medical-AI
vocabulary becomes trading vocabulary, and it explains several things the 2026
timeline only shows the finished form of.

## The through-line: premium is his unit, and has been since Feb 2025

| date | quote |
|---|---|
| 2025-02-26 | "the **premium traded** is already t[wice] …" on NVDA positioning |
| 2026-04-01 | Thinkscripts comparing buying vs selling premium, `Premium = Cost × Volume` |
| 2026-03-24 | "high **GEX-Volume ratio**" as a screen |

**This forces a correction to something I claimed yesterday.** I framed his
`lot size > 10` screen as a rule I had improved on by thresholding on premium.
Reading eighteen months, that is not what the lot filter is: **it selects which
book to look at** (limit order book, regular monthlies, institutional lots),
while **premium is the measure he applies to it**. He uses both, for different
jobs. My matched-selectivity test is still valid and its numbers stand — a size
threshold and a premium threshold select genuinely different prints (32% overlap,
+17 points of premium captured) — but "I improved his rule" was the wrong
framing. We were measuring different objects.

## The origin: an Expert System, not a trading model

**2025-05-30** — "FIRST GLIMPSE: An Algorithmic Stock-Picking **Expert System**.
Thesis: using **data mining and a scenario analytic model**, associations between
a set of **disparate attributes** …"

*Expert system* and *scenario analytic model* are CDSS terms. The design he
brought over is: combine heterogeneous evidence into a decision, not fit a
predictor to a target.

**2025-05-31** — "Human bias removed. **Left tail clipped**." Two preprocessing
steps stated as steps, which is the CDSS habit of making the pipeline auditable.

## The interface pattern we do not have

**2025-06-01** — "For the week of May 27, within the top 50 most active ETFs,
show me those with: • bullish measures of options sentiment, • price volatility
below 10%, and • **dark pools short sale volume consistent with accumulation**."

He states a multi-criterion screen **in domain language** and the model executes
it. Our brief is one-way: the machine emits a fixed report and the human reads.
There is no path for Andy to ask *this* question of *this* data without me
writing a script.

## The framework we have none of: dark pools

**2025-02-27** — "Market **fragility** has increased substantially … SPY options
traders bearish by end-of-session. **Dark pools shifted from distribution to
liquidation**."

Dark-pool volume carries labelled regime states — accumulation, distribution,
liquidation — and he treats them as a framework peer to the auction and the
chain. We have zero dark-pool data. This is a fourth framework, and unlike
resting size it needs a vendor.

## Auction primacy predates everything

**2025-09-30** — "Auction market process is alive and well. Truthfully … **the
only edge you ever need**."

Stronger than the April 2026 line we built the precedence rule on. He has held
this since at least Sept 2025.

## Higher-order greeks

**2025-12-31** — "busy refining an AI-based analysis of **higher order Greeks**."
We have gamma and charm. Higher-order means vanna (∂delta/∂vol) at minimum. On a
day when IV moves and spot does not, vanna is the term that explains the hedging
flow and neither of ours does.

## What was built from this

**Low-volume nodes** (2025-10-01: *"LOW volume points are often key levels …
key decision points"*). We computed VPOC and the value area — where volume piled
UP — and had nothing for where it did not. An LVN is a place to expect **speed,
not support**.

Building it produced a measurement finding worth more than the feature:

| bar size | avg price span | deepest dip found |
|---|---|---|
| 30 secs | 1.99 pt | 5.3% of peak |
| 1 min | 2.87 pt | 32.5% |
| 5 mins | 6.39 pt | 41.9% |
| 15 mins | 11.84 pt | 64.3% |

**Spreading a bar's volume across its range is a moving average whose width is
the bar's price span, and it fills troughs in exact proportion.** Our published
profile uses 1-minute bars. For VPOC — a peak — that is harmless. For LVNs — a
trough — it is structurally hostile: the approximation fills exactly what we are
looking for.

And then the honest part. On 2026-08-07 the function returned **zero** nodes, and
investigating it confirmed the function was right: the one 5.3% dip sat at index
6 of 45 with 8% of peak to its left — a tail, where the day ended, not a gap the
auction crossed. The three genuine interior dips came in at 37.8%, 39.2% and
50.1% against a 25% cut. **The day had no gap; the threshold was not wrong.**

The temptation there is obvious and I did not take it. Lowering the cut until
nodes appear is how a free parameter becomes a finding. Instead `profile_gaps()`
now reports every interior dip threshold-free, so "nothing qualified" and "the
cut is too tight" can never again look identical:

```
Low-volume nodes .... 0 below the 25% cut; deepest interior dips
                      7,783 (33%), 7,757 (40%), 7,772 (51%)
```
